# Changelog

## [Unreleased]

### Changed (the contact page left the menu, and the hero headline stopped taking two lines)
- **Contact is out of the header menu, and its form is at the foot of the help centre** – Asking for help and reading the answers were two separate destinations, so the visitor who found nothing in the FAQ had to leave the page to say so. The support cards and the message form are now one component, `components/sections/ContactBlock.tsx`, rendered both by `/[locale]/contact` and at the end of `/[locale]/help` under "Didn't find a solution?" — one implementation rather than a second copy of a form that posts to a live endpoint. The page itself is untouched and still reachable: the footer links to it, its metadata and canonical are unchanged, and putting the header entry back is one line in `Header.tsx` and one in `lib/navigation.ts`, or an entry added on the admin navigation screen. The "still need help?" box at the top of the help centre was then removed altogether — with the form now at the foot of the same page, a box above the questions telling you where to ask was pointing at something two screens down; `help.needHelp` and `help.contactCta` stay in the message files for whoever wants it back. The "check the help centre first" tip is dropped from the copy that renders on the help centre, where it would be pointing at the page you are already on, and the floating chat button is hidden behind `SHOW_STICKY_HELP` in `StickyHelpButton.tsx` — one word to bring it back.
- **The homepage headline was set to 60 px inside a 536 px column** – "Connected worldwide!" therefore broke across two lines that towered over everything beneath them. The size is now fluid and stops where the longest of the three headlines still fits one line, which is around 2.4 rem at desktop; deliberately without `whitespace-nowrap`, so a future translation that outgrows the numbers wraps to two balanced lines rather than forcing a horizontal scrollbar across the whole hero. The wording is untouched. `heroSubtitle` gained a trailing ellipsis in all three languages.

### Security (hardening found while reviewing the above)
- **The contact form had no upper bound on any field** – `name` and `message` had minimums and no maximums, against a `Text` column on a public endpoint. They are now capped at 100 and 5000 characters in `contactFormSchema`, matching the `maxLength` on the inputs so the ceiling is reached by the field refusing characters rather than by an English validation message appearing in a Hebrew form.
- **The auto-reply could be aimed at somebody else's inbox** – The one thing the contact endpoint sends to an address a stranger chose was limited per IP only, so rotating addresses could put our branded mail into one person's inbox repeatedly. It is now also limited to three per recipient per day, keyed by a hash so the rate-limit table does not accumulate email addresses. The submission is still stored and the admin still notified when the limit is hit; only the courtesy copy is withheld.

### Fixed (Ticket 037 — a customer could pay and be told nothing was wrong)
- **An order was marked `COMPLETED` whether or not an eSIM had been provisioned** – The Paddle webhook set the final status on payment, so a fulfilment that never produced a profile still looked finished to the customer, to the admin list and to every report built on it. The status is now `COMPLETED` only when a profile actually exists, and stays `PROCESSING` otherwise, which is the state the delayed-order email already describes.
- **Retry could buy a second eSIM for an order that had already bought one** – Both retry routes, the customer's and the admin's, called `purchasePackage` without checking whether the order already held a supplier batch id. They now fetch the existing profile when `esimOrderId` is present, and refuse a `PROCESSING` order that has no batch id rather than guessing. `PROCESSING` orders became retryable at the same time, since they are the ones that need it.
- **A refund or chargeback left the order looking like a completed sale** – `adjustment.created` and `adjustment.updated` are now handled and mark the order `REFUNDED`, idempotently, so a reversal is visible where the sale is.
- **The account area hid the orders most in need of explanation** – A `PROCESSING` order was absent from My eSIMs and unexplained in Orders. It now has its own block saying what the state means, with a "check again" button, and the client no longer reads the retry route's 202 as success: a reply carrying `pending: true` says so instead of claiming the eSIM is ready.
- **Nobody had agreed to anything at the point of payment** – The checkout now has a consent checkbox linking to `/terms` and `/refund`, enforced server-side in `create-transaction` before any Paddle call and recorded in the transaction's `custom_data` with a timestamp. The validation messages in `travelerInfoSchema` became keys rather than English literals, which is what was previously shown to a Hebrew customer who left a field empty.

### Fixed (Ticket 036 — the FAQ contradicted itself, and the site never gave the one tip that matters)

The answer now says a deleted profile generally cannot be reinstalled from the same QR on any device, offers to ask the provider with an order number in hand, and promises nothing; `howItWorks.trouble4Desc` says the same.
- **"Valid for 180 days before activation" was ours, not the provider's** – Nothing in the supplier's package fields or in `PackageOverride` carries a pre-activation figure, so the number could not be substantiated for any individual plan. The answer now explains that validity starts at the first connection at the destination, that a per-plan installation window exists and is set by the provider, and invites an email for a specific plan.
- **Top-ups were advertised and cannot be bought** – There is no top-up path in the purchase flow. The answer now says so and points at the thing that does work: buy another plan and install it alongside the first.
- **The golden tip is now on the site and in the purchase email** – The five-step, after-you-land sequence that resolves almost every "it isn't connecting", as a highlighted block at the head of the troubleshooting section, with the early-attach warning stated explicitly because switching the line on at home can start the validity period early. The same five steps and the same warning replace the old one-line tip in the post-purchase email, word for word in all three languages, so the email and the page cannot drift apart. `trouble1Desc`, `trouble3Desc` and `faq.answerNoSignal` were aligned to the same order.
- **Five questions a first-time eSIM buyer actually asks were missing** – Data-only, whether their own number keeps working, carrier-locked phones, "No Service before I fly — is that normal?", and what happens when the data runs out. Appended rather than inserted, because the homepage renders the first five of the same array. The data-only line also sits on the plan detail page under Add to cart, where the buying decision is made rather than only in the FAQ.
- **The help centre was twenty questions in one undifferentiated column, and the structured data was a second hand-maintained copy of it** – The list is grouped now, installation and troubleshooting first, and `help/page.tsx` derives its JSON-LD from the same array. The two had already drifted: the page rendered a question the structured data did not know about.

### Fixed (Ticket 020 follow-up — the browser was still offering an app)
- **A visitor on mobile was still being asked to install Sim2Me** – Ticket 020 deleted our own install banner and the `/[locale]/app` marketing page, and the FAQ now states plainly that no separate app is needed. But the site still met every one of Chrome's installability criteria — a manifest with `display: standalone`, 192/512 icons, and a service worker with a `fetch` handler — so the browser generated its own install prompt over the top of us, which reads to a customer exactly like the thing we had just told them does not exist. The manifest now serves `display: browser`, which removes installability; name, icons and `theme_color` still serve for branding, and the favicon comes from `metadata.icons` and is unaffected. `appleWebApp.capable` is now explicitly `false`, which exists only to make an installed instance open chromeless. That one is worth recording as a trap: simply deleting `capable: true` was not enough, because Next defaults it to `true` whenever the `appleWebApp` object is present, so `mobile-web-app-capable: yes` was still in the served `<head>` — caught by reading the built HTML rather than the diff. The separate `other: { 'mobile-web-app-capable': 'yes' }` was a duplicate of the same tag and is gone. The service worker stays: it is not what gates the prompt, and it is what gives an offline visitor a page instead of an error. The manual "Add to Home screen" item in the browser's own menu remains and cannot be suppressed — Chrome offers it for every site. The standalone shell at `/app/` and its own manifest are untouched; nothing in the UI links to it.
- **A customer could pay and hear nothing at all** – The purchase email was sent inside `if (firstProfile)` in the Paddle webhook. If the profile never arrived within the retry window, or if anything in fulfillment threw, the only message that went out was an admin alert; the person who had just been charged got silence, which is the shape of a chargeback. Both paths now send a new order-delayed email — localized in Hebrew, English and Arabic — carrying the order number, what was bought, what was paid, and a plain statement that the team is on it. It deliberately contains no error text: an English exception fragment inside a Hebrew apology tells the customer nothing they can act on. The underpayment branch still tells the customer nothing, by explicit decision, and its code is byte-identical to before; it returns before any customer-facing send is reachable.
- **The one email a buyer did get was not a receipt** – It carried the plan name, data, validity, QR and activation details, and nothing else: no order number to quote to support, no amount, no currency, no date, no ICCID. Every one of those was already on the `Order` row at the moment the email was built and simply not passed in. All five callers now pass them, and each row is conditional on its own field, so a caller that genuinely lacks one omits the row rather than printing an empty one. No destination row, though the field exists: `Order.destination` holds whatever the supplier returned in `location || locationCode`, which is sometimes "Turkey" and sometimes "TR", and the plan name beside it already reads "Turkey 1GB 7Days".
- **The buyer's language was thrown away after checkout** – The locale reached the webhook through Paddle `custom_data`, was used once, and was never stored. Every later send — admin resend, customer retry, admin retry — therefore fell back to Hebrew, including for buyers who had never seen a word of it. `Order.locale` is now persisted at webhook time and at internal-sale time, and read by all four later senders. The column is nullable with no default, so the nineteen existing orders read `null`, which `toEmailLocale` maps to Hebrew — bit-for-bit what they get today.
- **The QR code was invisible to anyone who does not click "show images"** – Gmail and Outlook block remote images by default, so the single email that matters could open with no QR code in it. It is now attached as a file as well as embedded, fetched server-side behind a five-second timeout and a size ceiling; any failure sends the email exactly as before. The supplier's PNG is used rather than re-encoding the activation payload with the installed `qrcode` package, because a QR we generated ourselves is a way to hand somebody a code that does not match their eSIM.
- **The contact form posted to a hardcoded personal address** – `to: ['gavriel.kr@gmail.com']`, so form submissions landed somewhere the site's other admin notifications did not. Now `ADMIN_NOTIFICATION_EMAIL`, like everything else.

### Fixed (Ticket 033 — one language per email)
- **The verification and login-code emails stacked all three languages in one body** – Everything else was localized; these two were not, because their call sites carried no locale and the earlier decision was that threading one through registration to translate four sentences was not worth touching auth for. It was the wrong call: a customer who registered on the English page received a message that opened in Hebrew, repeated itself in English, then again in Arabic, and looked machine-generated in all three. Both now have a copy table keyed by locale, like the five templates that already did, and the locale travels from the page the action started on using the pattern `forgot-password` established — the client sends it in the body, the route reads it through `toEmailLocale`. Six call sites in total: register, resend-verification, and the three OTP routes, plus the `OTP_RESTORE` block in `auth.ts`, which is commented out today but would otherwise have come back speaking Hebrew to everyone. Both functions default to Hebrew when no locale is passed, so a caller that was not updated behaves exactly as it did.
- **A password reset could succeed and still leave the customer locked out** – `reset-password` cleared the token and wrote the new hash but never touched `emailVerified`, and sign-in refuses an unverified customer. Somebody who registered on the site, never received the verification email, and reasonably concluded they had mistyped their password would set a new one, be told "Password updated. You can now sign in", and be refused with the same error as before, with no remaining screen to try. Opening a link sent to an inbox proves ownership of it exactly as the verification link does — the same reasoning already written beside `emailVerified: true` in the Paddle webhook — so a completed reset now marks the address verified. The token is 32 random bytes, expires in an hour and is cleared on use, so this recognizes verification that has already happened rather than offering a way around it. Buyers were never affected; the webhook has always marked them verified.

### Fixed (Ticket 033 — the delivery blocker, now cleared)
- **`sim2me.net` sends its own mail** – The domain is verified on a Resend account of its own, separate from the one carrying `valentyns.com`, since the free plan allows a single domain and one API key serving two businesses is a key whose leak costs twice. DKIM, SPF and the feedback MX are live on `send.sim2me.net`; the root MX is untouched, so Cloudflare Email Routing still delivers to `info@sim2me.net`. Worth recording that `_dmarc.sim2me.net` was already published at `p=quarantine` by a previous registrar, which means the DNS work was not optional decoration: the first mail sent from the domain without correct DKIM would have gone straight to spam. Verified end to end rather than assumed — all sixteen templates plus a smoke test were sent to a real mailbox and each one confirmed `delivered` against the Resend log, the first mail in the project's history to leave `@sim2me.net` for an address other than the account owner's.

### Known blocker — RESOLVED 2026-08-03 (Ticket 033, not a code fault)
- **No customer has ever received an email from this site** – Found while preparing the previews, and confirmed against the full send history of the Resend account production uses: across 100 records going back to 2026-05-12, every email ever sent went to one address, the account owner's, and every one was sent from `onboarding@resend.dev` — Resend's sandbox sender, never `@sim2me.net`. Sixteen completed orders belong to eight distinct customers; seven of them, four of whom look like real buyers and two of whom paid today, have never been sent anything. `sim2me.net` has no Resend DNS records at all — no DKIM at `resend._domainkey`, no `send.sim2me.net`, and an SPF pointing at Cloudflare rather than Amazon SES — so the domain cannot be verified, and an unverified Resend account may only deliver to its owner. Everything else is rejected with a 403, which `sendEmail` catches and swallows by design so that a mail failure can never fail an order. That is why sixteen orders completed and nobody noticed. This is DNS and dashboard work, not code, and none of the above reaches a customer until it is done.

### Added (Ticket 032 — Selling an eSIM from the admin panel)
- **A package can now be sold to a customer without a checkout** – Until now there was exactly one way for a customer to get an eSIM: pay by card on the site. That left no answer for a deal closed on the phone, a customer compensated after a fault, a replacement for an eSIM that never worked, or someone who paid by Bit or bank transfer. `POST /api/admin/orders/internal` buys the package from eSIMaccess for real — the wholesale price leaves the prepaid balance exactly as it does on a paid order — writes a `COMPLETED` order against the customer, and sends the QR email. From the customer's side it is indistinguishable from a purchase they made themselves. Reachable from a package card in eSIM Packages and from the Orders header of a customer's panel in Accounts; the same modal serves both, locking whichever half the caller already knows and searching for the other.
- **An account can be opened during the sale** – If the email has no account, the modal says so and offers to create one with a name and a phone in any country code, using the existing `PhoneInput`. The temporary password it generates satisfies the site's own strength rule, so the customer can actually sign in with it and then change it, and the account is marked verified because sign-in blocks unverified customers and an unusable password in a welcome email is worse than none. The customer is created before the order and before the purchase, so if anything about it fails, nothing has been bought.
- **`Order.source` and `Order.idempotencyKey`** – `source` is `PADDLE` for every order that existed and for every order checkout will ever write, and `ADMIN_INTERNAL` for these; the orders list shows an `INTERNAL` tag beside the status pill so a sale that never went through a till is never mistaken for one that did. `idempotencyKey` is unique in the database and generated once when the modal opens, which is what makes a double-click or a retry return the original order instead of buying a second eSIM. Both columns are additive with defaults, and the migration SQL was read before it was applied.
- **The price is floored at the supplier's cost, server-side** – The field opens at cost, so the floor is also the default, and the route refuses anything under it with the live wholesale price in the message rather than trusting the number the browser sent. A gift is therefore booked at cost rather than at zero: revenue gains money that never arrived, and the deliberate trade is that no order can ever be recorded below what it cost to fulfil. The balance is checked before anything is written, and `Fee cost` and `Net in bank` stay untouched by design, since both are Paddle-only and an internal sale has no transaction to charge a fee on.

### Fixed (Ticket 032)
- **Retry could have bought a second eSIM for an internal sale** – `/api/admin/orders/[id]/retry` calls `purchasePackage` for any order that is not `COMPLETED`, without looking at whether the order already holds a supplier batch id, and the Retry button is offered on `PROCESSING`. An internal sale whose purchase succeeded while its profile was still arriving sits in exactly that state, so one click would have spent the balance twice on an order already paid for. Retry is now hidden for an `ADMIN_INTERNAL` order that already has an `esimOrderId`; the condition for Paddle orders is byte-identical to before, because the fulfillment route is a money path and this was not the ticket to edit it in. The same hole remains reachable for a Paddle order parked in `PROCESSING` with a batch id — pre-existing, recorded in the ticket, and waiting on its own approval.

### Fixed (Ticket 029)
- **Hebrew deal cards read as two bare numbers** – `10 GB · 30 ימים` puts a Latin unit inside a right-to-left line, where it becomes its own left-to-right island and leaves the two figures side by side with nothing to say which one is the data and which one is the days. Plan cards had localized the unit for a while (`ג'יגה` / `جيجا`); the deal surfaces had not. The helper moved out of `PlanCard` into `lib/utils.ts` and is now applied everywhere a data amount prints beside a duration: the deals row, the hero offer card, the hero deal chip and the add-to-cart toast.
- **The deal card on a destination page linked to that page** – "All {destination} deals and plans" made sense on the homepage and in the hero, and nowhere else. The destination page now shows the deal as a plan card, which has no such link.
- **Destination pages quoted more than checkout charges** – A package on offer showed its catalog price on `/destinations/{slug}` — Australia at $9.40 while the homepage sold it at $8.64 — and checkout then charged $8.64 anyway, because `getActiveDealPrice` resolves the deal server-side. So the page overstated the price and hid the discount on exactly the page the homepage deal links to. The deal is now applied server-side to the plan it belongs to, may only ever lower a price, and is wrapped in its own try/catch so a deals failure cannot take a destination page down. `fromPrice` is computed from the final prices, so the header and the page metadata cannot contradict the card below them.

- **Regenerate could overshoot the configured count** – `regenerateTodayDeals` deletes today's unpinned rows and then calls the generator, which built its slot counter from zero and knew only about *yesterday's* pins. - **Which deals a day serves was not deterministic** – `ensureTodayDeals` read the day's rows with no `orderBy` and then sliced them to `count`. - **Pinned carryover could burn a slot on a duplicate** – The carryover loop pushed a row for a package that might already exist for the day, relying on `skipDuplicates` to drop it at insert time — by which point it had already consumed one of the day's slots. It now skips such a package before taking the slot.
