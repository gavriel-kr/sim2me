"Global phone number" sounds like a number that belongs to the whole world. That does not exist: every phone number belongs to one country, set by its country code. What does exist, and what this plan gives you, is one number that works almost everywhere you go. Land in Tokyo, move on to Bangkok, finish in Lisbon, and your number never changes. No SIM stall at every airport, no new number to hand out every few days.

This guide covers what you actually get, where the number works and where it does not, which plan suits which kind of trip, and how to pair it with a cheap data plan so you pay less overall.

What a "global phone number" really is

The global number is a US number, with the +1 country code, delivered on an eSIM. In each country the eSIM roams onto local networks that the provider has agreements with. According to the provider's network list at the time of writing, that means T-Mobile and Verizon in the United States, EE and Three in the UK, KDDI and SoftBank in Japan, and AIS and TrueMove H in Thailand.

Because the number is American, two things follow:

  • Callers dial a US number. For friends and businesses in the United States, calling you is a domestic call. For a hotel in Thailand, it is an international call, exactly as if they were calling your number from home. It is not a Thai number.
  • The number goes wherever you go. That is the real advantage: one number on your business card, your bookings and your email signature for the whole trip, or the whole year.

The money-saving setup: global number plus a data plan

The data in the global plans is fine for light use. Heavy users pay less by splitting the job between two eSIMs:

  • The global number eSIM handles calls and SMS. This is the line whose number you give out.
  • A data-only eSIM for the country you are in handles the internet. For example, 5GB for 30 days in Japan cost about $5.40 when this guide was written.

In your phone's settings, choose the data-only eSIM as the mobile data line. The global number line keeps receiving calls and texts. This needs a phone that can keep two eSIM lines active at once: iPhone 13 and later, and many recent Android phones. With this setup, the smallest global plan (1GB for a week) is usually enough, and the heavy browsing runs on the cheap plan.

Keeping your number: renew before the end

You can renew the global number from your account on our site before the plan ends. The renewal is added to the same eSIM, with no new installation, and the number stays the same. We email you a reminder with a renewal link 48 hours before the end. If the plan runs out without a renewal, the number is cancelled and cannot be recovered. If clients or suppliers have your number, do not leave it to the last day.

  • You need a number from the country you are in: the global number is always a US number. For a local number, a local SIM is the answer.
  • You make lots of calls: minutes are limited. For long calls home, use WhatsApp, FaceTime or another app over data.

Frequently asked questions

Which country is the global number from?

The United States. It is a +1 number that works in 168 countries. Anyone calling you dials a US number, wherever you happen to be.

What happens when I cross a border?

The eSIM connects to a local network in the new country and your number stays the same. It can take a few minutes after crossing; if there is no signal, restart the phone.

Can I use the global number in the United States?

Yes, the United States is on the list. If your trip is only to the US, though, the US plan is much cheaper.

Can I choose my number?

No. The number is assigned automatically and appears in your phone's settings once the eSIM is installed.

How long can I keep the same number?

As long as you renew before each plan ends. The longest single plan is 365 days, and renewals keep the number.
