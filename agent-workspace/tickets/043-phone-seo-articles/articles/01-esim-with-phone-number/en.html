What you get with an eSIM that has a phone number

An eSIM is a SIM card in digital form. You install it by scanning a QR code, with no plastic card and no shop visit. A data-only eSIM gives you a data allowance and nothing else: no number, no regular calls and no SMS. An eSIM with a phone number bundles four things into one plan:

  • Data: a fixed allowance, just like a data-only plan.
  • A phone number: a real number assigned automatically when the eSIM is activated. You cannot choose it in advance.
  • Call minutes: an allowance that depends on the plan. On the French number, calls within Europe are unlimited.
  • SMS: also set by the plan.

The number is not sent to you by email. It lives inside the eSIM, and you will find it in your phone's settings once the eSIM is installed. On iPhone go to Settings → Mobile Service (or Cellular) → the eSIM line → My Number. On Android go to Settings → About phone → SIM status → Phone number. The exact wording varies by manufacturer. Five situations where a number earns its price

1. People need to call you

Hotels, holiday rentals, car hire desks, tour operators and couriers still run on phone numbers. Give them a number from home and many of them simply will not call it, because for them it is an international call. A number they can dial easily removes that barrier.

2. You cross several borders

On a multi-country route, one number that works everywhere saves you from buying a local SIM at every border and handing out a new number each time. The global number works in 168 countries. The French number covers 36 European countries, including the UK, Switzerland and Norway.

3. You work while you travel

Freelancers, remote workers and anyone dealing with clients abroad need a number that stays the same, one they can put in an email signature or on an invoice. The US and global numbers can be renewed before they run out, so the number stays the same trip after trip.

4. You want a US number

Both the US plan and the global plan come with a +1 number. For friends, family or businesses in the United States, that number is a domestic number, not an international one. Our guide to getting a US phone number on an eSIM explains the difference between the two plans.

5. The internet is not enough

A regular call goes over the mobile network, not the internet. When data is slow, at a crowded festival or in the countryside, a normal call often still gets through. Some countries also restrict calls made through apps, and there a regular call is sometimes the simplest way to reach someone.

When you do not need one

If your plan for the trip is messaging apps, maps and social media, a data-only eSIM will cost less and do the job. And if you are moving to a country for a long stay, a local SIM from a local operator, with a number from that country, is usually the better choice. Our numbers are built for travellers and people who move between countries. They are not a replacement for a permanent local line.

What happens to your normal number?

Nothing. The eSIM is added as an extra line. It does not replace your home SIM, so calls and texts to your usual number keep arriving, and the phone shows two lines. To avoid roaming charges from your home carrier, keep Data Roaming off on your home line, turn it on for the eSIM line only, and choose the eSIM as your mobile data line.

Newer phones can hold several eSIM profiles and keep two lines active at once (on iPhone, from the iPhone 13 onwards). That lets you stay reachable on your home number while you use the eSIM for data and local calls.

US and global numbers can be renewed from your account on our site before the plan ends. The renewal is added to the same eSIM, with no new installation, and the number stays the same. We email you a reminder with a renewal link 48 hours before the end. If the plan ends without a renewal, the number is cancelled and cannot be recovered. Before you buy: there are no refunds after purchase, because the eSIM is delivered immediately. Make sure your phone supports eSIM and is unlocked, and choose the plan by the number of days you really need.

Four mistakes to avoid

  • Installing too early. Validity starts at installation, not when you land.
  • Deleting the eSIM to tidy up. The QR code works once. After deletion you cannot simply scan it again.
  • Buying for a locked phone. A phone locked to one carrier will not accept another provider's eSIM, even if the installation seems to succeed.
  • Expecting to pick the number. Numbers are assigned automatically. You cannot choose a memorable one or move an existing number onto the eSIM.

Frequently asked questions

Can people call me on the eSIM number?

Yes. It is an ordinary phone number that anyone can dial. Give it to your hotel, driver or rental company and they can call you like any other number.

When do I find out what my number is?

The number shows in your phone's settings once the eSIM is installed. On iPhone: Settings → Mobile Service (or Cellular) → the eSIM line → My Number. On Android: Settings → About phone → SIM status.

Is the global number a local number in each country?

No. The global number is a US number (+1) that works in 168 countries. People in the country you are visiting will be calling a US number.

What is the best way to make long calls home?

For long calls with family, use WhatsApp, FaceTime or another calling app over the plan's data. Keep the included minutes for ordinary calls, such as your hotel, a driver or a local business.

Can I get a refund if I do not use the plan?

No. The eSIM is delivered immediately after payment, so there are no refunds after purchase. If your eSIM was not delivered because of a fault on our side, contact us with your order number and we will sort it out.
