The US may be the country where a local number is most useful. Americans still lean heavily on ordinary calls and text messages. Walk-in restaurants put you on a waitlist and text you when your table is ready. Car rental firms, hotels, valet stands and pharmacies send texts to US numbers. Airport shuttles and pick-up drivers call when they are outside. From a foreign number some of those messages never arrive, or the other side's system simply will not send them.

This guide explains which plan suits which kind of trip, how it works alongside your home SIM, and what to do if your trip crosses into Canada or Mexico.

Which plan for which trip?

A city break

A week in New York, Miami or Las Vegas: 5GB for $13.90 covers maps, messaging and social media, and 50 minutes is plenty for restaurants, hotels and drivers.

A road trip out West

Two weeks through California, Nevada, Utah and Arizona with navigation running all day: 20GB for 15 days at $45.90. In national parks such as Yosemite, Zion and the Grand Canyon, large areas have weak or no signal on any network, so download offline maps before you drive in.

Parents visiting children in the US

Parents who fly over to spend a month or two with a son or daughter studying or working in the States need a number the family, the doctor's office and the neighbours can call. The 30-day plan ($49.90, 20GB) covers a month, and it can be renewed before it ends to keep the same number for a longer stay.

Families with teenagers

Give everyone a US number and you can call and text each other just as you do at home. It is useful at malls, theme parks and stadiums, where mobile data tends to buckle exactly when you need it. The $7.90 weekly plan is enough for kids who mostly browse on Wi-Fi.

Frequent visitors

The 365-day plan at $108.90 gives you a US number that is waiting for you on every trip. It only works inside the US. If you want a US number that also works at home, you need the global plan; see our guide to getting a US phone number on an eSIM.

Crossing into Canada or Mexico

The US number works only inside the United States. If you are also heading to the Canadian side of Niagara, to Vancouver or to Cancún, the global number is the better fit. It is also a +1 number, and it works in the US, Canada and Mexico plus 165 other countries. According to the provider's network list at the time of writing, it uses T-Mobile and Verizon in the US, Bell, Rogers and TELUS in Canada, and Telcel in Mexico. It costs more (from $27.90 a week), but you keep one number across every border.

Using it alongside your home SIM

The eSIM is added as a second line; your home SIM stays in the phone. Before you land:

  • On your home line, turn off Data Roaming so your carrier does not charge you for data abroad.
  • On the eSIM line, turn on Data Roaming and set it as your mobile data line.
  • On iPhone, set the eSIM as your Default Voice Line so calls in the US go out from your US number.

Once installed, your number is in Settings → Mobile Service (or Cellular) → the new line → My Number. If it is not there, see how to find your eSIM phone number.

Just need data?

If you do not need a US number, a data-only plan for the US costs less: 5GB for 30 days was about $6.60 when this guide was written. It suits travellers who are happy with messaging apps and do not need to be called. Unsure? Read our data-only vs phone number comparison.

Before you buy: your phone must support eSIM and be carrier-unlocked. Validity starts on the day you install, so install on the day you fly or the day before. There are no refunds after purchase.

Frequently asked questions

Does the US number work everywhere in the US?

It works wherever the network has coverage. In remote areas and national parks, signal is weak on every provider.

Can I use it in Canada?

No. The US number works inside the United States only. For trips that include Canada or Mexico, choose the global number.

When does the plan start?

On the day you install it. Install close to your flight, not a week before.

Can I keep the number for my next trip?

Yes, if you renew from your account before the plan ends. The 365-day plan suits people who visit several times a year.

How many minutes do I need for a week?

For most travellers, the 50 minutes on the 5GB weekly plan are plenty. For long calls home, use a calling app over data.
