Who actually needs a US number?

Businesses selling to Americans

Freelancers, agencies and startups outside the US often find that American clients are happier to call a +1 number than an international one. Put it in your email signature, on your website's contact page or in your professional profiles, and answer it from wherever you are. The global plan makes that work from 168 countries.

Americans living abroad

If you have moved overseas and dropped your US carrier, the global plan gives you a +1 number that works in your new country and on your travels. Family and friends in the US call it as a normal domestic number. The 365-day plan works out at about $16 a month.

Visitors to the United States

Drivers, restaurants, hotels and rental desks in the US work with US numbers and send ordinary text messages. With a local number you deal with them like anyone else. For a trip, the US plan is cheap and does the job. Our USA eSIM with a phone number guide covers trip planning in detail.

Students and new arrivals

In the first weeks after landing, before you have set up a long-term US contract, you need a number for your landlord, your school and your employer. A 15- or 30-day US plan gives you a working number from the first day.

What a +1 number means for the people who call you

  • Callers in the US dial a domestic number. No international rates, no country code, no app.
  • Callers in other countries dial a US number, which is an international call for them, just as it would be to any US number.
  • You receive the call wherever the plan works: anywhere in the US on the US plan, in any of the 168 countries on the global plan.

Setting it up

  1. Check your phone supports eSIM and is carrier-unlocked. See the compatible devices list.
  2. Buy the plan and receive the eSIM by email with a QR code.
  3. Install it at the right time. Validity starts on the day you install. Install the US plan shortly before you fly. The global plan can be installed at home if your country is on its list.
  4. Find your number in your phone's settings. On iPhone: Settings → Mobile Service (or Cellular) → the new line → My Number. More in our guide to finding your eSIM number.
  5. Keep your usual SIM as a second line if you like. When abroad, turn off Data Roaming on it and use the eSIM for data.

Keeping the number

Both the US and the global number can be renewed from your account on our site before the plan ends. The renewal is added to the same eSIM and the number stays the same. We email a reminder with a renewal link 48 hours before the end. If a plan runs out without a renewal, the number is cancelled for good, which matters if clients or family rely on it.

Worth knowing: numbers are assigned automatically. You cannot pick an area code or a specific number, and you cannot move an existing US number onto the eSIM. There are no refunds after purchase.

Frequently asked questions

Can I get a US number that works outside the United States?

Yes. That is the global plan: a +1 number that works in 168 countries. The cheaper US plan works only inside the United States.

Do people in the US pay international rates to call me?

No. For them it is a call to a US number, so a domestic call, wherever you are.

Can I choose a New York or California area code?

No. The number is assigned automatically and you cannot choose the area code.

How long can I keep the number?

For as long as you renew before each plan ends. The longest plan is 365 days, and renewals keep the same number.

Does my current number keep working?

Yes. The eSIM is added as a second line and does not replace your existing SIM.
