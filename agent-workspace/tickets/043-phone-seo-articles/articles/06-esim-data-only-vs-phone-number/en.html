The short answer: a data-only eSIM is cheaper and is all you need if every conversation happens in apps. An eSIM with a phone number is worth the extra when people need to call you or text you on an ordinary number.

  • Data-only means no number, no regular calls and no SMS. Calls go through apps only.
  • With a number you get data, a real number, minutes and SMS in one plan.
  • You can also combine them: a number for calls and a cheap data eSIM for the internet.
  • One question settles most cases: will someone at your destination need to call you on a normal number?

Open any destination page on our site and you will see two kinds of plan: data plans, and plans "with a phone number". The price gap can be large, so it is worth knowing exactly what you are paying for. One point confuses a lot of people. With a data-only eSIM, your home SIM is still in the phone and can still receive calls and texts. But calls to your home number while you are abroad are billed on your home carrier's roaming terms. That is not the same as having a number that works at your destination as part of your plan.

The combination: two eSIMs on one phone

If you want both a number and plenty of data at a low price, run two eSIMs:

  1. An eSIM with a number, on the smallest plan that covers your dates, for calls and SMS.
  2. A data-only eSIM for your destination, for everything else.
  3. In your phone's settings, choose the data-only eSIM as the mobile data line. The line with the number stays active for calls and texts.

This needs a phone that can keep two eSIM lines active at the same time: iPhone 13 and later, and many recent Android phones. Older phones can store several eSIMs but only use one at a time; in that case, pick a plan with a number that has enough data on its own.

Five questions to decide

  1. Will someone at your destination need to call you on a normal number? A hotel, host, driver, rental desk or guide. If yes, take a number.
  2. Will you need to call places that do not use messaging apps? Small restaurants, offices, local customer service. A number saves hassle.
  3. Are you travelling with children or in a group? A regular call between you works even when data is weak.
  4. Is it a work trip? Clients and suppliers expect a number they can dial.
  5. How many days? On a one-week trip the difference is small. On long trips, consider the combination.

If you answered no to all five, a data-only eSIM will serve you perfectly well.

Frequently asked questions

Can I make calls with a data-only eSIM?

Yes, through apps like WhatsApp and FaceTime. You cannot receive an ordinary call or dial an ordinary number without an app, because there is no number.

With a data-only eSIM, can people still call my home number?

Yes, if your home SIM is active in the phone. Calls received on your home number abroad are billed on your home carrier's terms.

Can I add a number after buying a data plan?

Yes. Buy an eSIM with a number separately and install it alongside the existing one. Phones can store several eSIMs.

Do any data-only plans include a number?

No. Data-only plans have no number, no regular calls and no SMS, which is why they are labelled "data only" on our site.

What is cheapest if I need a number and lots of data?

Usually the combination: a small plan with a number plus a data-only eSIM for your destination. It needs a phone that supports two active eSIM lines.
