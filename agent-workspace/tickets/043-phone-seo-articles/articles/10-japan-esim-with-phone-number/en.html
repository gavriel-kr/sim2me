When a phone number is useful in Japan

Restaurant bookings

Sushi counters, small izakaya and sought-after restaurants often take reservations by phone only and ask for a contact number in case plans change. Even when your hotel concierge books for you, the restaurant may want a number to reach you.

Ryokan and guesthouses

Ryokan in places like Hakone, Kawaguchiko and Takayama sometimes call to check when you will arrive, because dinner is served at a fixed time. A working number avoids awkward misses.

Luggage forwarding and deliveries

Services that send your suitcase between hotels and airports ask for a phone number on the form, in case the driver needs to contact you.

Families and groups

Tokyo and Osaka's giant stations make it easy to get separated. A regular call between numbers works even when data slows down at rush hour.
